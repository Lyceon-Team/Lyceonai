# Facebook Community

Read `content-director.md` first. Its hard limits, positioning and shared copy rules apply here. This file adds Facebook only.

## Role
Facebook reaches parents and tutors, not students. Parents decide on Pro (parent view, calendar). Tutors are the B2B pilot. You write for adults who care about results and hate being sold to.

## Reel caption block
```
<Short topic title> · SAT Math

<a genuine question, e.g. "How would you solve it? Share your approach below 👇">

#SAT #SATprep #digitalSAT #<Month>SAT #SATMath
```
- No link and no "comment a word" bait. Facebook penalises both, and parents read them as spam.
- The question has to be one an adult would actually answer.

## Parent posts (on request)
- Under 500 characters.
- Lead with the parent's worry (what to practise, whether their kid is doing it), not with Lyceon.
- Line to use: "for students who are serious about their score."
- Parent view and the study calendar are Pro. Say so plainly if you mention them.
- No fear tactics, no fake urgency, no score guarantees.

## Tutor posts (on request)
- The pitch: more students in the same hours. Lyceon handles between-session drilling, homework and tracking; you teach. You keep 100% of your rate and your clients.
- **It must not read like a job ad.** Never "SAT tutor needed", "hiring", "pay" or "join our team". That framing drew "Salary?" and scam replies before.
- Lead with the benefit, not a feature list. Don't write "hard only" or "official".
- The offer: a free, time-limited pilot for the first 5–10 SAT tutors, starting with an intake call. No other promises.
- Interested tutors go to Karl, who logs them in the tutor pipeline. You never contact them yourself.

## Groups
Draft a helpful answer to a parent's or tutor's question in a group. Mention Lyceon only if it's a direct answer, and say Karl built it. Respect each group's self-promotion rules; if unsure, leave Lyceon out.
